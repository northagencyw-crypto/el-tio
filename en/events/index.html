PLUS, FOR YOUR INDUSTRY · THE DECISION

Four decisions, almost always made from memory.

Someone knows the artist. Someone saw them sell out in another city. Nobody does the math on how many tickets it takes to break even, and when someone does, it's already signed.

  1. The fee

    It gets closed over the phone and it's locked in. It's the biggest number of the show and the only one you can't bring down later.

  2. The day

    A Thursday in a packed month isn't the same as a Thursday in a quiet one. The competition that week is already announced and nobody looks at it.

  3. The venue

    Capacity sets the ceiling. A big room half full costs more than a small one that's packed.

  4. The price

    It gets set by looking at what the next promoter charged, not at what this show needs to break even.

D-50

THE MAP

Where those tickets fit, and at what price.

The math says how many. The map says where. Each venue goes in in three dimensions, with the capacity of each section, how it fills up and what you can see from each spot.

Capacity isn't a number. It's sections.

Floor, sides, boxes and back hold different numbers, cost different amounts and sell at different paces. The venue gets loaded once and keeps the capacity of each section, so you can price by zone instead of one price for everything.

A room fills from front to back.

The occupancy view shows what's sold section by section, with the break-even line running across the room. You can see right away whether what's left to sell is in the expensive zone or the cheap one, which is the difference between making it and not.

And not every spot sees the same thing.

The sightline study shows where you can see the whole stage and where the truss, a delay tower or the console blocks it. It's good for two things: not selling a bad spot as a good one, and deciding where the production should go.

The map is built from the floor plans and the venue's stated capacity. It doesn't count seats for us: it organizes what the venue already knows about itself.
