Member retention for gyms and studios: cancellations and class bookings

A member doesn't leave the day they cancel. They leave weeks earlier, when they stop showing up. This guide explains what research says about attendance and cancellations, how to use class bookings as a signal, and how to set up WhatsApp follow-up that doesn't sound like a collections call.

Cancellation starts before they cancel

At gyms and studios, cancellations show up in the billing system, but they start with attendance. The most cited research on the subject is by Stefano DellaVigna and Ulrike Malmendier, who followed 7,752 members of three gyms in the United States for three years. Members on a monthly fee of more than 70 dollars went an average of 4.3 times a month, and ended up paying more than 17 dollars per visit when they could have paid 10 with a ten-visit pass. The study also documents that they were slow to cancel.[1]

A more recent paper, using data from a gym chain in Turkey, found that about half of new members didn't keep up their attendance streak at six weeks, and by seventeen weeks it was eight in ten. The gaps between visits grew before the streak broke.[2] It's a preprint, not yet peer reviewed, and from another country, but it points the same way: the signal is there weeks before the cancellation.

Class bookings are your best signal

At a yoga, Pilates or cycling studio, every booking is an attendance data point. Read well, bookings warn you before a declined payment does:

SignalWhat it usually meansWhat the system does
New member with no second visitThe habit didn't takeMessages them to book the next class
Booked and didn't comeSomething came up or they lost interestOffers another time that same week
Stopped booking their usual time slotTheir routine changedAlerts the coach or front desk so a person reaches out
Full waitlistNot enough classes at that timeNotifies when a spot opens up
Declined paymentIt could be the card, or it could be a cancellationSends the payment reminder and flags the member as at risk

The exact timing depends on each business. A studio where people come twice a week notices an absence sooner than a gym where they come once.

What the agent should answer

Most of the messages a gym or studio gets are about schedules, spots and plans. A WhatsApp agent answers them at any hour and logs every booking:

  • Today's schedule and open spots in each class.
  • Booking, changing or canceling a class, with the cancellation policy in plain view.
  • A trial class for people asking for the first time.
  • Current plans and prices.
  • Pausing or canceling a membership: the agent takes the request and passes it to a person.
WhatsApp · 21:50 Hi, is there a spot in reformer tomorrow at 7? Hi. I'm the studio's AI assistant. Tomorrow at 7:00 there are 3 spots left. Should I save you one? Yes, please. Done, you're in reformer tomorrow at 7:00. If you can't make it, let me know before 22:00 tonight and I'll free up your spot for someone else. Sample conversation.

WhatsApp's rules

If the member messages you, you have 24 hours to reply with free-form messages. The reminder for tomorrow's class or the message to someone who stopped coming usually goes out outside that window, and for that you need approved templates and the member's permission.[3] The WhatsApp Business policy asks you to respect anyone who asks to stop receiving messages.[4]

In practice: ask for permission at sign-up (“Can we send your class reminders here?”). And when someone stops coming, the first message asks what happened. A promotion at that moment sounds like a collections call.

Health data and contracts

Many gyms ask about injuries, medical conditions or pregnancy at sign-up. Mexico's 2025 personal data law treats data that can reveal someone's health as sensitive, and to process it the law requires the person's express written consent, with a handwritten signature, an electronic signature or another authentication method.[5] That data belongs on the form the member signs, not in a WhatsApp conversation, and the agent doesn't need it to book a class.

On contracts, Profeco (Mexico's consumer protection agency) recommends that consumers look up the registration number of a standard-form contract and compare it with the registered one.[6] If your gym uses a standard-form contract, check with your lawyer whether you need to register it. And put in writing in the contract how a member cancels: the agent can take the request, but the process has to be clear.

How it connects with what you already use

  • Bookings and access. If you already use a booking system or access control, the agent checks open spots and the CRM reads attendance from there.
  • Pipeline. Prospect, trial class, member, at risk and canceled. Every member at their stage, with the person following up.
  • Billing. The payment reminder goes out automatically, with the text you approve, and stops once the payment is recorded.

How to tell if it's working

  1. New members who come back in their first week.
  2. Bookings where the member was a no-show.
  3. Members flagged as at risk and how many booked again.
  4. Cancellations for the month and how long each member had been with you.
  5. Time to first reply for people asking about a trial class.

If today you only find out about cancellations when a payment doesn't go through, that's the first finding.

Mistakes to avoid

  • Following up with members only when the payment fails. By then they haven't come in for weeks.
  • An agent that won't let people cancel. If canceling a class is hard, the spot sits empty and someone else misses out on it.
  • Asking for medical data on WhatsApp. It goes on the signed form, with their consent.
  • The same message for everyone. A three-year member is not the same as a three-week one.
  • Hiding that it's an AI. Saying so in the first message avoids misunderstandings.
