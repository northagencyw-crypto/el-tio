First organize, then automate

Automating a messy process makes it faster, not better. That's why the Audit first looks at how each task gets done today, who does it and where it gets stuck. Only then do we decide what's worth automating and in what order.

What gets automated usually meets two conditions: it happens many times a week, and it follows rules that can be written down.

The Monday report

Every week, management gets a summary put together from the systems the business already uses: new contacts and where they came from, how many got a reply and how fast, appointments or reservations, closed sales and pending payments. It arrives by email or WhatsApp, without anyone having to put it together.

It's for one thing only: deciding on Monday what gets done that week. That's why it's short.

FAQ

What systems does it connect to?

To what you already use, as long as it has some way to connect: an API, an export or an email that always arrives in the same format. If a system doesn't let you get data out, we tell you in the Audit before we quote.

What happens if an automation makes a mistake?

Anything that involves money or an important customer goes out as a draft for a person to approve. We only let something run on its own once we've tested it and reviewed it with you.

Who maintains the automations?

We do, as long as you have the monthly service. Your team gets a guide so they know what each workflow does and who to tell if something changes.
